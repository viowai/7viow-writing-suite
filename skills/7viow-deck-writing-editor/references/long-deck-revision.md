# Long-deck Revision

Use the branch matching the requested change. [Narrative and Page Writing](narrative-and-page-contract.md) remains the source for page responsibilities, reading mode, title/body layers, and summaries. This reference handles changes across pages and chapters; sentence and paragraph editing remains Writing Editor's responsibility when that support is needed.

## Expand the understanding chain

1. Read the full requested range and the later passages that depend on it. Trace what the audience must understand from the opening question through the final conclusion. Locate where a named process, concept, or service is only introduced and where its workings are actually explained. Finish this pass with the missing understandings and their dependent passages identified; a topic's presence in an overview is not evidence that its explanation exists.
2. Allocate depth to those gaps. Cover the full requested range, giving more space to unfamiliar mechanisms, consequential distinctions, and actions the audience must be able to follow. Already established ideas can remain brief. For a process, follow it through its later stages so the audience can understand what changes and how one stage enables the next. For an offer, explain the service scope the later value claim relies on. Finish when each part has an adequate place in the proposed sequence, including the less prominent parts.
3. Write the missing explanation before deciding how many pages it needs. Add a page when the audience needs a distinct explanation, comparison, example, transition, or room to read it. Give the added page a job in that chain; a noun in the source does not earn a page by itself. Finish when the revised manuscript supplies every understanding the dependent passages assume, with emphasis proportionate to need.

For example, a purchasing deck may show the whole workflow, explain supplier selection, then jump to a rollout calendar. If the calendar assumes the reader understands receiving checks, handling defects, and releasing payment, explain those stages first. The overview establishes orientation; the added explanations make the calendar intelligible.

For a revised close, use the summary responsibility in [Write the page](narrative-and-page-contract.md#write-the-page) after the body is settled.

## Compress around one audience task

1. Identify pages serving the same audience question and the unique meaning each contributes. Separate repeated framing from conditions, causal links, examples, responsibilities, actions, and distinct service items that the audience still needs. Choose the smallest grouping that can perform the shared task coherently. Finish when the proposed merge has a clear reader purpose and each unique contribution has a destination.
2. Write the actual merged content, including the necessary transition or speaker support for its reading mode. Combine repeated material and preserve unique contributions beside the claims they qualify. Return one conservative revision by default; produce alternative versions only when requested, in the requested number. Finish with usable merged copy, rather than only a page-count proposal or instructions for a later writer.
3. Compare the merged copy with the source pages and briefly account for the unique meanings: retained in the combined passage, moved to a named destination, or removed for a stated reason consistent with the task. A destination must contain the material, not merely promise to receive it. Finish when the audience can still recover every necessary distinction and the surrounding sequence reads continuously.

When removing “extra explanation,” judge what the passage does. Cut repeated setup or an explanation whose work is already done nearby. Preserve the causal link, concrete action, example, or scope detail that makes a label understandable; shorten its expression where useful. A list of surviving headings does not demonstrate that their meanings survived.

For example, two equipment-support pages may repeat the same service introduction while one explains eligible devices and the other names the repairs included. A merge can remove the second introduction while retaining both the eligibility conditions and the repair list. Replacing both with “comprehensive support” loses the reader's ability to judge the offer.

## Continue from manual edits

1. Read the current edited original and identify the dimensions the user has established: wording, proposition, content, page grouping, or another named choice. Use the applicable edited passages as anchors for the requested continuation. Treat earlier drafts as comparison material where needed. Finish when the current source and the dimensions to carry forward are clear.
2. Apply the change at its actual level. For title or paragraph-lead changes, use [Write the page](narrative-and-page-contract.md#write-the-page) and retain supporting passages that already work. When an edit changes a concept's meaning, follow it through affected explanations, examples, role descriptions, transitions, and conclusions. Finish when every affected passage expresses the new meaning consistently and unrelated content remains intact.

For example, changing a logistics proposal from “same-day delivery” to “dispatch on the day of confirmation” changes the service promise. Update the related timeline and customer example as well as the title. By contrast, a sharper headline over an unchanged service promise can leave the established body intact.

## Revise chapters in sequence

1. When the user has requested serial chapter work, carry that sequence forward through ordinary intermediate steps. For the next chapter, gather its current original, applicable accepted anchors, the current change request, and only the preceding or following passages needed to understand dependencies. Finish preparation when the chapter can be revised without guessing an input or reopening settled choices.
2. Complete the chapter's requested revision before moving to the next. Preserve its full intended coverage and carry forward any dependency the next chapter must honor. A useful chapter return is the actual revised text plus the changes or unresolved inputs that matter downstream. Finish when the requested chapter change is visible in that text and its necessary connections are identified.
3. Integrate completed chapters by reading their joins: what the previous ending establishes, what the next opening assumes, and whether terminology, repeated explanations, or moved material still work. Repair the affected joins and references. Finish when the assembled deck carries the argument continuously while retaining completed chapter content outside those repairs.

Split work according to the material that must be held together and the actual editing load. A chapter may need smaller coherent units; tightly coupled passages may need to travel together. Use a genuinely separate execution context when isolation is needed, and pass the relevant text and decisions into it. Headings within one running context organize work but do not clear that context. Page counts and token thresholds do not determine a universal split, and user-visible task creation follows the user's instruction.
