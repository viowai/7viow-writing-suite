# Claims and Copy

Use for factual review, quantitative material, Ask pages, evidence placement, or disputed wording.

## Claim strength

Distinguish supported facts, bounded observations, user judgments, hypotheses, business decisions, planned actions, demonstrated results, and unsupported assertions. Preserve the distinction in the wording. Source-backed inference is allowed when its chain and uncertainty are clear; it is not a new observed fact.

A shorter sentence must not add an actor, broaden a population, strengthen certainty, change a denominator, or turn a future plan into a result. Compare the actual source and revision for who acts, what action is conditional, and which people or stage the condition covers. Keep important dates, exceptions, comparisons, and attribution. A prerequisite for one activity does not automatically restrict a broader activity or an adjacent stage. In graphic relationship notes, preserve the same distinction between sequence, parallel work, and permission to proceed.

When detail is missing, distinguish an explicitly undecided matter, information absent from the supplied material, and an established rule whose details are not supplied. Absence alone does not establish that a rule is unwritten or an activity has not occurred. Preserve the supported status without filling the missing detail.

## Screen, speech, author, production

Audience copy contains the supported argument. Speaker support adds oral context and transitions. Production advice serves the executor's requested operation. Keep author working notes out of the reader-facing delivery. Apply the [copy-use check](prose-quality-gate.md#正文用途) to every displayed section; a source gap does not automatically become a sentence for the audience.

Include and label the sections needed for the requested handoff, keeping them together by page. Do not append drafting explanations or move them into narration, appendices, internal editions, or delivery notes.

## Claim review

For a central, quantitative, comparative, causal, or disputed claim, examine the challenges that apply:

- counterexample;
- alternative baseline;
- causality;
- transfer beyond the source's scope;
- temporal freshness;
- denominator or cohort;
- conflicting sources.

Use actual evidence and reasonable questions, not invented counter-facts or simulated audience testimony. A challenge is resolved only when the evidence covers it. If a decision-critical challenge remains, qualify, downgrade, remove within scope, or escalate the claim. Stop the strong claim, not unrelated supported writing.

If a structured review is requested, give claim, source scope, applicable challenges, evidence-backed responses, residual issue, and disposition (`retain`, `qualify`, `downgrade`, `remove`, or `needs-evidence`). Otherwise a concise reasoned judgment is sufficient. Do not pretend omitted or inapplicable checks passed.

## Quantitative and time-sensitive material

Preserve metric, unit, denominator, cohort, baseline, period, transformation, and uncertainty where relevant. A chart title cannot imply causality or representativeness that the data does not establish. If a necessary basis is missing, explain what the data can still show and what it cannot support.

Preserve source precision in prose and structured handoff fields. A month, month-end, phase or approximate quantity must retain that scope rather than become a confirmed calendar day or exact value. Keep the factual cutoff separate from generation and verification timestamps. If a required format needs a precise value, retain the source meaning and identify the conversion convention or unresolved value where the consumer needs it.

Use the source's as-of period or relevant version for changing facts. Refresh them when the task claims current status; otherwise label the historical scope. Conflicting sources need a reasoned resolution or a bounded claim, not an automatic preference for the latest date.

Use visible source locators and material qualifiers suited to the reading mode. Full references may sit in an appendix or notes, but essential limitations must remain available where the claim is read.

## Ask and decision pages

Distinguish three operations:

1. Extract an existing Ask: preserve the actual page meaning and identify missing information without inventing it.
2. Draft an Ask from a brief: use confirmed requester, requested party, action, timing, prerequisites, and evidence ceiling from the brief or consumed sources. The page sentence need not already exist.
3. Perform an external action: apply the user's actual action authorization separately. Writing an Ask or approving copy alone does not send it.

An incomplete field blocks only the part that depends on it. A short content draft may omit unknown optional timing rather than print an empty schema. Use a six-field record when the user requests structured extraction or a real downstream interface requires it. Reuse applicable external-action authorization already granted in the task; do not reset it because the turn changed.

## Copy repair

Headlines state a takeaway; bullets add distinct support. Use the smallest repair that fixes the actual defect. Keep meaningful comparisons and intentional repetition. For close-read or dual-use material, speaker-only evidence cannot rescue an unsupported visible claim.

Report findings with location, observed defect, audience consequence, and a specific repair. Distinguish misleading facts or unauthorized actions from a narrative break and from optional polish. A source or qualifier failure cannot be offset by stronger style.
