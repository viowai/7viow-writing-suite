# Sources, Decisions, and Routing

Read when using supplementary sources, resolving unclear approval, or coordinating ownership. Ordinary page writing can use the supplied brief directly.

## Identify the task

Name the current deck or excerpt, relevant version, audience, reading context, intended decision or understanding, and requested action. Use paths, slide labels, section names, dates, and actual excerpts to locate material. Keep historical drafts, source evidence, visual examples, and the current writing target distinct.

A style or visual sample does not become factual authority. A completed export does not establish content approval. A new filename alone does not supersede a prior decision.

## Use the governing sources

Read the portions needed before claiming alignment. For a substantial deck, keep a concise source map: source location and version, material read, its use, and important exclusions. Do not create a consumption form for every ordinary sentence.

Keep the selected version's disclosure scope when reading supplementary evidence. Use it to verify, understand, or complete the requested content; a true detail omitted from that version is not by itself an error. Distinguish correcting a known error from adding information or restoring a deletion. The latter requires the current task to authorize that expansion, even when the supplementary source is more detailed.

When sources conflict, determine whether the disagreement concerns facts, wording, sequence, or another dimension. Apply the user's clear current decision to its scope. If facts remain unresolved, qualify or hold the affected claim and continue supported material.

## Interpret approval

Use the user's actual words and their clear referent. “Use this revised ending and keep the rest” is actionable for the identified version; it does not need an additional form. Praise or a user edit alone does not approve all other content. Ask only when ambiguity affects the requested action.

Content approval applies to its stated scope and remains valid until relevant changes or revocation. Reopen affected content, not every page. Applicable operation authorization also persists; a new turn does not reset it.

## Choose ownership and coordinate support

Deck owns its agreed content unit in the requested page system: the audience movement, sequence, on-screen argument, slide-bound speaker support and content handoff. An explicitly requested isolated voice exercise can be led by Partner; an isolated sentence edit can be led by Editor. The presence of a slide does not make every local action a full deck assignment.

If the user asks for all three skills on a deck, keep one Deck content owner. Partner contributes expression or voice choices for named units; Editor contributes located language/fidelity findings or scoped repairs. Read the current material and accepted decisions before either contribution. Use only support that resolves an actual issue, without requiring all roles on every request.

Keep accepted audience, page count or order, exact wording, source limits and visual instructions within the user's declared scope. A contributor may propose reopening a dimension with a specific reason; a new preference is not permission to replace the structure. The owner integrates authorized changes and checks affected transitions, screen/speech consistency and qualifiers once. A remaining factual conflict blocks its claim, not independent work.

Pass the actual excerpt or current source location, reader and reading mode, accepted choices, preserved factual limits, exact change requested and the next useful action. Natural-language decisions remain effective within their scope. A role change does not reset approval or require a new intake, form or task.

A content candidate may be delivered without claiming user approval. State whether a handoff enables review, an authorized trial or production. Visual design, native mutation, rendering and distribution use the selected executor and the user's actual task authorization. Sensitive source material remains within its requested purpose; reading history does not authorize modifying the archive.
