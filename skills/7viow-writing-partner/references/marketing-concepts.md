# Develop Marketing Concepts

Use this reference to develop marketing angles, campaign lines, thematic series, or new audience and occasion variants from a supplied offer or brief. It supports both exploration through feedback and a complete candidate set in one delivery.

The work is to find a reason this reader would care and make it felt in actual copy. Keep the supplied product, business decisions, accepted voice, and requested scope as the working brief. A campaign concept can express one part of an offer without redefining the whole brand.

## Find the human meaning

Identify the job of the requested writing: explain the offer, evoke a desire, invite an action, or help someone choose. These jobs can coexist across a campaign; a line has limited room. A product description may need the process first. An invitation may work better through the moment that makes the process worth undertaking.

Use the available material to find a promising entry:

- **Relationship:** whom the reader wants to help, understand, impress, thank, join, or be understood by.
- **Desire:** what they hope to do, gain, express, change, earn, or keep.
- **Moment:** when that desire becomes noticeable, such as an introduction, a return home, a birthday, a first attempt, or a familiar task.
- **Action:** what someone can actually do that connects the offer to that meaning.

Start where the brief has energy. These are lenses, not required fields in every sentence or a matrix to exhaust. A physical object, a remembered remark, a small decision, or a recognizable exchange may carry several at once. Commercial ambition, independence, pleasure, belonging, care, and recognition are all possible motives; choose from the brief rather than assigning each demographic a stock emotion.

Follow the connection far enough to write it: what would this reader care about, where would they feel it, and how does the offer plausibly enter that moment? Product facts can support or delimit the idea without becoming a list of features in disguise. If the connection rests only on a generic good outcome that almost any product could claim, find a more specific action or situation in the material.

For example, a photo-book tool supporting photos, captions, and printed books can invite someone to preserve a family story or mark a year in their own life. “给家里那些讲过很多遍的故事，留一个翻得开的地方。” expresses the first idea through a familiar relationship and object. It does not need to report a real customer's story.

This part is complete when there is a usable angle and actual language showing why the offer matters to the intended reader. A theme label alone leaves the creative work unfinished. A clear brief permits drafting directly; use a focused question only when a missing fact changes the angle or available action.

## Distinguish a new concept from an adaptation

Compare candidates by the meaning they offer, not their labels or vocabulary. A meaningful difference can come from another desire, relationship, action, or moment. The same person may have several reasons to care; a new profession name may add none.

For the photo-book example, preserving a shared family story and privately taking stock of one's year are different concepts. Adapting the family-story concept for the person making the book and the relative giving it as a gift can also be useful: the core idea continues, while the decision, relationship, and language change.

Keep those two contributions legible:

- **Concept development** supplies another reason to care or another meaningful way to encounter it.
- **Audience or occasion adaptation** makes a useful concept work for a particular reader and use.

Judge adaptations by their fit. Preserve a valuable one even when its concept is shared. When the request is for distinct directions, strengthen or combine candidates whose differences are only cosmetic. Within an intentional series, repetition can create recognition if each line contributes a particular situation or meaning.

Let the request set the coverage and volume. Where the user asks for more, extend what matters before reaching for increasingly remote audiences. A useful series is complete when its promised differences appear in the copy and the requested audiences or occasions are served; category count alone cannot establish that.

## Make the feeling understandable

Give the reader enough action and context to understand the emotional turn. Dialogue, imagery, and omission can carry feeling, but the reader should be able to tell what the central object is, what happened or is imagined, and why the response matters. Supply the missing connection rather than automatically removing the expressive device.

“把‘终于有空了’变成‘原来你还记得’” leaves the cause between the two remarks unclear. “翻出那张旧合照，约多年没见的朋友再拍一张。” provides an action and a relationship through which the feeling can emerge. These illustrate the difference in comprehension; the second is usable only in a brief where that invitation fits.

For feedback such as “sarcastic,” “too intimate,” or “roundabout,” inspect who appears to be speaking to whom and what the sentence asks that person to believe. A recommendation phrased as “Let's see if you can do it” may sound like a challenge. Adding “I will always be beside you” changes the relationship and introduces a commitment. Repair the intended gesture instead: make the friend's support for the idea or next action clear at the level of closeness the brief supports.

Directness and brevity are separate judgments. Add a necessary actor or object when it removes a guess. Cut explanation that delays the point when the action and meaning already survive. Keep a concrete detail that carries the feeling, even if a shorter version is possible. Read the revised line as a whole: the repair should resolve the named problem while retaining the intended relationship, force, and invitation.

## Match the action to the role

Determine who receives the message, who acts, and who benefits. They may be different people. A prospective user can try or join; a supporter can fund or encourage; a recommender can introduce; a host can consider cooperation; a viewer can attend or respond. Use only the actions available in the brief.

Adapt what the reader is choosing and why it matters to them. Addressing a parent, colleague, or partner requires more than changing the noun in a participant's line. The supporter may care about seeing someone become independent; the participant may care about doing something they can call their own.

An action can sit in the headline, supporting copy, button, or surrounding placement. Keep the overall message intelligible without forcing the same opening or a full call to action into every line. Writing for a possible partner or viewer does not establish that cooperation, an event, or access is available. If that fact is unresolved, keep the candidate's intended use clear beside the copy and name the specific dependency.

## Preserve imagination and distinguish claims

Read the whole expression in its intended placement to decide what it asks the audience to believe:

| Expression | Creative room and boundary |
|---|---|
| Desire or invitation | Imagine a future moment the reader may want. Its usefulness does not depend on a past customer having lived it. |
| Image, metaphor, or imagined dialogue | Create a symbolic scene or recognizable exchange. Keep its imagined identity; presenting it as a testimonial or observed event changes the claim. |
| Rhetorical exaggeration | Preserve the intended expressive force. Judge whether the context turns it into a literal assurance about timing, reach, or results. |
| Product fact, evidence, or service commitment | Carry forward what the brief supports. New eligibility, access, deliverables, deadlines, guaranteed outcomes, or obligations require a business basis. |

Neither a particular verb nor an emotional tone settles the category. “Imagine” cannot disguise a concrete promise, and an invitation does not automatically promise that every reader will achieve its imagined future. Respect accepted intensity while keeping this distinction clear.

Review a specific expression when its likely reading changes a material claim. Resolve the issue in the wording or place a consequential condition where the audience needs it to decide. Keep unresolved source needs beside the draft. This is a targeted writing judgment, not a sentence-by-sentence evidence dossier or a disclaimer attached to every imaginative line.

## Deliver the writing

Return the actual lines or passages requested, with enough grouping or brief explanation to make the meaningful choices visible. When authorized to choose and complete a set, finish it within the brief. A request for ideas does not inherently require another interview or approval round.

Finish when the copy supplies a recognizable reason to care, the central action and relationship are understandable, the requested conceptual differences or adaptations are present, and the language stays within the offer's factual and service boundaries. Preserve the accepted voice and wording where they apply. For continuing feedback and candidate-state handling, use [collaboration-loop.md](collaboration-loop.md); for a chosen rhetorical movement or a repair within a sound concept, use [writing-methods.md](writing-methods.md) only as needed.

Author selection establishes a preferred version. Audience comprehension and commercial performance require their own evidence when the task asks for those conclusions.
